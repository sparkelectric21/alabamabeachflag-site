Coastal first aid

Jellyfish Stings: What to Do

General first-aid information summarized from the American Red Cross. This page does not replace emergency medical care.

On this page

  1. When to call 911
  2. Immediate care
  3. Continue monitoring
  4. Official source

Emergency warning

Call 911 for serious symptoms

Get emergency help for trouble breathing, signs of a severe allergic reaction or shock, a sting to the face or neck, a sting from an unknown marine animal, a known potentially lethal jellyfish sting, or any other situation that appears life-threatening.

Immediate care

  1. Leave the water safely. Help the person get out as soon as possible while protecting yourself.
  2. Remove visible tentacles carefully. Do not touch them with bare skin. Use a gloved hand, a hand protected by a plastic bag or towel, or a blunt tool such as a stick or plastic utensil.
  3. Flush with seawater when needed. If those removal options are not available, use seawater to rinse the area. Do not rub the sting.
  4. Use hot, non-scalding water for pain. Immerse the affected area in water that is as hot as can be comfortably tolerated for at least 20 minutes, or until the pain is relieved. Take care not to burn the skin.
  5. Avoid pressure and friction. Do not rub the area or wrap it with an elastic bandage.

Continue monitoring

Watch for changes in breathing or responsiveness and for signs of an allergic reaction or shock. Provide only the care you are trained to give, and seek emergency help when serious symptoms are present.

Source: American Red Cross

This page is a concise paraphrase of current American Red Cross jellyfish-sting guidance. Alabama Beach Flag did not author or medically verify that guidance.
